## Problem

After `rtt-stt-engine`, other participants' speech arrives as one mixed loopback stream labelled
"Unknown". Discord already tells Orbolay who is speaking and when (`SPEAKING_START`/`SPEAKING_STOP`
over IPC, and the `speaking` flag in websocket voice-state updates). We need to use those events to
put the right name on every line.

### Overview

This spec turns "Unknown" into real names. Discord already tells Orbolay when each person starts and
stops talking. **Proposal** keeps a short history of those moments and, for each transcribed
segment, picks whoever was talking during it. **Design** explains why "most overlap" beats "who
spoke last", and why your own mic is always labelled as you. **Risks** covers people talking at the
same time, which gets an honest joint label rather than a guess.

## Proposal

- `SpeakingTimeline` in `orbolay-core`: per-user list of `[start, end)` intervals on the pipeline
  clock, pruned after 60 s.
- IPC handler (`SPEAKING_START`/`SPEAKING_STOP`) and websocket handler (`speaking` transitions)
  record into the timeline, in addition to their current behaviour.
- Attribution for loopback segments: pick the user whose speaking intervals overlap the segment
  most (excluding the local user). If two or more users each cover ≥35% of it, label the line
  "Name A + Name B". If nobody overlaps, keep "Unknown".
- Mic segments are always the local user (`AppState.user_id`), whatever the timeline says.
- Latency compensation: shift Discord events by a configurable `rttEventOffsetMs` (default
  measured in spec 10 of epic `rtt-human`, formerly source task 2.2) to align event time with audio
  time.

## Out of Scope

- Splitting overlapping speech into separate lines per speaker (needs source separation or
  per-user streams; see the epic's Out of Scope).
- Voice-print diarization for non-Discord audio.
- The manual 3-person call in IPC and websocket modes that tunes the threshold and offset (source
  task 2.2). It needs real participants, so it moved to spec 10 in epic `rtt-human` (id 7).

## Design

- The timeline lives in `orbolay-core` and is fed by transports unconditionally (a few intervals per
  user, pruned). This avoids `#[cfg(feature)]` noise in the transport crate, which is easier to review.
- Majority-overlap rather than "who started speaking first". VAD segments often include a trailing
  word from the previous speaker, and overlap is more robust to that.
- Discord's speaking events and the audio we capture both pass through the client's jitter buffer,
  but events may arrive slightly earlier than the played audio. A single constant offset is good
  enough, and it's configurable for edge cases.
- The IPC handler already delays `NotSpeaking` by 300 ms for UI smoothing. The timeline records the
  *raw* event time, not the delayed UI state.

## Alternatives Considered

- Using `voice_users[*].voice_state == Speaking` snapshots at segment end. Rejected: it's a point
  sample, loses everything said before the last change, and inherits the 300 ms UI delay.

## Open Decisions

- Joint-label threshold (35%) and default event offset: tuned from the manual test now owned by
  spec 10 (formerly task 2.2). This spec ships the 35% threshold and a configurable offset.

## Risks

- Websocket mode may batch or delay speaking updates differently from IPC. Mitigation: measure the
  offset per transport mode, with separate defaults if needed.
- Users talking over each other are common in games. ACCEPTED: joint labels are honest, and per-user
  separation is out of scope.

## Handoff

- Base branch `develop`, after `rtt-stt-engine` is merged. Segments carry pipeline-clock start/end.
- The IPC speaking handling is at `crates/orbolay-transport/src/ipc/ipc_message_handler.rs`
  (`SPEAKING_START` / `SPEAKING_STOP` arms).
- The pipeline epoch must be created before transports start recording, or recorded intervals must
  convert from `Instant` at read time. Prefer storing `Instant` and converting lazily.
- Task ids keep the source plan's numbering (`specs/plans/rtt-speaker-attribution.md`); 2.2 moved to
